Can you convert a bungalow or chalet bungalow loft in Lancing?

Usually, yes, and a bungalow is often the friendliest house on the street to convert, because it has roof space over the whole footprint and a hall to put a staircase in. What decides it is the roof structure, the head height under a wide span, and how much steel the roof needs before a bungalow loft conversion in Lancing can carry a floor. This guide is the order a surveyor works in.

01The roof structure decides the job

Two bungalows on the same road can be completely different jobs depending on how the roof was built. Everything else in this guide follows from that.

RoofHow it behavesWhat it means for a conversion
Trussed rafters, typically 1960s onwardsAn engineered set of small timbers working as one unit, with no member meant to carry a floorThe roof is opened by cutting out bracing and adding a designed floor and support structure, so a structural engineer is not optional
Traditional cut roofRafters, purlins and binders with space already between themEasier to open up, more usable volume, usually less steel
Attic truss or chalet roofDesigned with a room already inside the trussA conversion is often a finishing job rather than a structural one
Hipped roofSlopes in on the ends as well as the front and backLess height at the ends, so a hip-to-gable or a dormer recovers it

The surveyor's first hour is spent deciding which of these you have. If it is trussed, the design work goes up and so does the steel, and that is the honest reason two similar-looking bungalows can price differently.

02Chalet bungalows start from a better place

A chalet bungalow already has rooms in the roof, which changes the question from “can a floor go in?” to “what is missing?” Usually the answer is headroom at the eaves, a proper staircase rather than a steep companionway, and modern insulation where the original roof has little.

Where the chalet roof is steep and the first floor is genuine, the conversion is often a rear dormer to gain height across the back, or a re-roof that brings the insulation and ventilation up to current standards while the scaffolding is already up. Where the roof is shallow and the rooms are cramped, the decision is whether to raise the roof, and that is a planning question rather than a building one.

03Head height over a wide, low roof

Bungalows are wide, and a wide roof at a shallow pitch does not rise far. Before anything is designed, measure from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the ridge at the highest point. Around 2.2 metres is the working figure that leaves roughly two metres of room after a floor and insulation are in.

If the ridge is too low, there are three realistic routes: a rear dormer that lifts headroom over part of the plan, a hip-to-gable where the roof is hipped, or raising the roof itself. Raising the ridge goes beyond the householder roof right, which allows nothing higher than the existing roof, so it is a planning application. The fourth route, dropping the ceiling of the room below, sounds cheap and almost never is.

04Where the stairs land

This is a bungalow's advantage. A hall, a wide landing or the corner of a back bedroom usually gives a straight flight somewhere sensible, where a Victorian terrace has to fight for every centimetre. Two numbers govern it: the usual target under Approved Document K is 2 metres of clear headroom over the stair, and the floor below loses whatever footprint the stair takes.

Get this wrong and the conversion fails even when the roof is perfect. A bungalow with a central hall and a three-metre run is straightforward. A bungalow where the only route is up through the middle of the main bedroom is a different conversation, and it is better had before the drawings are finished.

05The planning question, which is local

A bungalow roof conversion may qualify for permitted development if it meets the applicable limits and conditions. Check the address and the complete design before committing to the work. Planning Portal guidance.

Two Lancing checks come first. The North Lancing conservation area removes the roof-addition right, so a bungalow inside it needs an application. Part of north Lancing is also inside the South Downs National Park, which changes who decides. Adur & Worthing Councils deals with building control either way.

06What the survey measures

  • Ridge height and the head height at three points along the roof, not one.
  • Roof construction, rafter size and spacing, and whether the roof is trussed or cut.
  • Ceiling joist size and span, and what will carry the new floor.
  • The route and headroom of a stair, and what the floor below gives up.
  • Chimney breasts, water tanks, boilers and anything already in the roof.
  • Party wall position, access for scaffolding, and where materials can be stored.

07The order the work usually runs in

The sequence is much the same on most jobs: design and structural calculations, then the building control application, then scaffolding and roof opening, then the new floor and stair, then the dormer or roof windows, then insulation, plaster, electrics, heating and finishing. A bungalow conversion usually runs for a few months once the structure is agreed, and the roof is open for the shortest possible part of that. Agree the programme in writing at the start rather than taking a verbal estimate.

Living in the house during the work is normal for a bungalow, because the loft is entered from outside or from a hall that can be screened. Agree the scaffold position, the skip position and the working hours with the company before the first delivery arrives.

Questions people ask

Can you convert a bungalow loft?

In most cases yes. A bungalow has roof space over its whole footprint, so the limit is head height under a wide, shallow roof and the strength of the existing roof structure, not the amount of space.

What does a bungalow dormer conversion involve?

A floor structure designed for the load, a stair into the hall or a back room, a dormer built out from the rear slope to give headroom, roof windows for light, and a full insulated and ventilated roof build-up, all signed off by building control at the end.

Does a bungalow conversion need planning permission in Lancing?

A rear dormer within 50 cubic metres, below the existing ridge and with matching materials is normally permitted development. Inside the North Lancing conservation area it needs planning permission, because the roof-addition right does not apply to conservation-area houses.

What if my bungalow roof is trussed?

It can still be converted. Trussed roofs are engineered as a single unit, so the design has to show how the new floor and the altered roof are supported, usually with steel. That is a structural engineering job, and it is the main reason two similar bungalows can price differently.

How much head height do I need in a bungalow loft?

Aim for roughly 2.2 metres between the top of the ceiling joists and the underside of the ridge. That gap absorbs the new floor and the roof insulation and still leaves about two metres of room. On a wide bungalow roof the answer usually decides whether the job is a dormer or a raised roof, so measure before anyone draws anything.
